ARRIT • THE HARBOR TERRACES

She saved the day that mended her family.

To keep it safe, she gave it away.

Now she cannot remember forgiving.

On Arrit, memories can be traded, sold, or entrusted to a keeper. The giver keeps the facts. The lived moment belongs to someone else.

Yarrow knows the afternoon she and her sister ended nine years of silence changed everything that followed. With her own memories beginning to fail, she placed that afternoon in the safest hands she could find. Now she can describe the reconciliation—but cannot remember becoming a woman who forgave.

The Dot is certain the memory should be returned. The keeper refuses. The sister will not ask. To understand why, the Dot must learn the difference between taking pain away and honoring the choice that made preservation possible.

Some memories survive only because someone agrees to miss them.

The question

How do you help someone living with the cost of the right decision?

A world with its own rule

Each Seer Worlds book is designed to stand on its own. The local problem belongs to the world first; the larger series mystery remains underneath the story instead of replacing it.

For curious readers

Short chapters, clear language, strange ideas, quiet humor, and enough mystery to keep the next world calling.
